Gigobiloma I See You — The Ground Before the First Word

Two forces organise all of reality. Not two enemies — two poles of the same engine. The battery needs both terminals. The heart needs both the contraction that pushes and the expansion that receives. Remove either and the system dies. Every tradition that has looked carefully at this has given the poles different names — yin and yang, masculine and feminine, the Hermetic principle of Polarity, love and fear — but the observation is always the same: reality is driven by the dynamic between two complementary forces, neither superior, neither complete without the other.

In the language of biology, these poles are the sympathetic nervous system (contraction — the body protecting itself, closing, sharpening) and the parasympathetic (expansion — the body connecting, opening, resting into relationship). In physics, they are emission and return. In consciousness, they are what every language has always encoded in its most basic distinction: the voiceless consonant, produced with the vocal cords at rest, precise and contained; and the voiced consonant, produced with the vocal cords vibrating, open and resonant. The love-pole is not love as sentiment. The fear-pole is not fear as weakness. They are the software and hardware of life — one gives direction, the other gives form.

Halu is built on this engine at the level of its atoms. Every voiced consonant in the language — b, d, g, v, z, j — is the expansion pole. Every voiceless consonant — p, t, k, f, s, c — is the contraction pole. This was not designed into the language. It was found there, already present in the physics of how the human mouth produces sound. What makes Halu different from every language that came before it is that this structure is made explicit, derived, and consistent at every level — from the individual phoneme to the longest possible compound word.

The language is organised around a periodic table of 60 base syllables: the intersection of 12 consonants and 5 vowels, each position carrying a specific force of meaning determined by where and how it is produced in the human body. The 60 syllables double as a base-60 number system — every word has a unique numeric value derived directly from its syllables' positions in the table, making every Halu sentence simultaneously a sequence of sounds, a sequence of integers, and a deterministic geometric standing-wave pattern in matter. The Æron calendar that accompanies the language derives its pulse unit from the tropical year divided by exactly 364, producing 13 months of 28 days each, zero accumulated drift by construction. Language and time share the same ground.

The first tier is the ground — not a rung to climb past but the substrate everything else rests on. Reality at this level is solid: bounded, locatable, undeniable. A stone is here and not somewhere else. A body occupies space. An action has a consequence. These are not philosophical propositions. They are the physical conditions of existence, and the practitioner at this tier has accepted them without reservation.

The law of Cause and Effect is the governing principle here — not as a spiritual concept but as the most practical discipline available. Every condition you inhabit is the crystallised consequence of prior actions: yours, and those of the people and forces that preceded you. Accepting this fully — without resentment about what you did not choose and without grandiosity about what you did — is the entire work of Tier 1. It sounds simple. Most people spend their lives avoiding it.

The vocabulary of Tier 1 is the vocabulary of the physical world: the body and its parts, the five elements, the names of things, basic actions, numbers, time units, kinship, and the most fundamental emotions as physical states — not as inner explorations (that is Tier 2's work) but as sensations in the body. Anger felt as heat in the chest. Grief felt as weight. Joy felt as lightness. The body knows before the mind names.

At Tier 1, every sentence is spoken from the physical register by default — the a-vowel domain: the widest mouth opening, the most locatable sound, the sound every human infant in every language makes first. You do not need to declare this register. It is the ground state. Everything else is a departure from it that must be explicitly marked.

Water seeks its own level. This is not a metaphor — it is the teaching of the second tier stated as physics. Every system with two poles and a fluid medium between them will, if left alone, find the midpoint: the place of maximum sensitivity, where the gradient is felt most clearly. The second-tier practitioner is learning to be that midpoint. Not neutral. Not passive. Maximally sensitive.

The Hermetic law of Polarity governs here: all things exist between two poles, and every quality exists on a continuous spectrum between its opposite. Hot and cold are the same quality at different degrees of the same scale. Love and fear — introduced at Tier 1 as the engine of reality — are now felt directly in the body as degrees of the same energy, not as two separate emotions. The practitioner who has done this work cannot be told they feel something they do not feel. Their body is too well-calibrated. The generative and receptive forces some traditions name separately — masculine and feminine, active and yielding — are understood at Tier 2 as one further expression of this same single axis: two poles of one spectrum, not two separate laws requiring a separate name.

The specific gift of this tier is what others call precognition and what it actually is: advanced interoception. The body measures emotional and relational fields continuously and largely unconsciously. At Tier 2, the practitioner has learned to read that measurement in real time — before the mind has processed it, before the other person has named it, before the situation has declared itself. Damasio's somatic marker hypothesis (1994) establishes the neurological basis: the body generates pre-conscious emotional signals that guide decision-making, and patients with damaged access to these signals make systematically worse decisions despite intact logical faculties. This ability is carried with care. To sense another person's interior state is to hold something they did not consciously offer.

Two new elements arrive at this tier. The vowel y — /ɪ/, the sound in "syllable" and "vinyl," the near-close near-front vowel sitting just inside i — expands the vocabulary of relationship and the in-between: a vowel close enough to i (Inner) to feel related, distinct enough to name its own territory. The consonant w (/w/) — the rounded bilabial potential, the wave-gesture of the lips forming and releasing — brings the vocabulary of possibility into the language. W enters here and not at Tier 1 because potential cannot be felt before the inner liquid state is accessible. To speak of potential without developed inner sensing is to speak of a country you have never visited.

Air is invisible but causally real. What you cannot see or hold is nevertheless shaping everything. At Tier 3, the practitioner inhabits the gaseous domain: the field between people, the social medium through which intention travels and compounds — and, more precisely, the first tier at which a pattern learned in one place is recognised operating, unchanged in its logic, somewhere else entirely.

The Hermetic Principle of Correspondence governs here: as above, so below; as within, so without. The law that shapes a cell's membrane is the same law that shapes a family's boundary, the same law that shapes a nation's border — one pattern, read at three different magnifications. A cell regulates what crosses in and out to survive; a household does the same with attention and trust; a society does the same with law. Nothing about this is metaphor stretched thin. It is the same governing structure, found again at a different scale, because reality does not invent a new rulebook for each size it happens to be. Tier 3 is where the practitioner stops treating the intimate and the cosmic as different subjects and starts reading one as a legible, nearer-scale version of the other.

The Yogic traditions found the same recursive structure in the body itself — the microcosm containing the macrocosm, as the Kybalion states it directly. The alchemical tradition built its entire method on this axiom: solve et coagula, the work done on the self mirrors and enacts the work done on matter. Every tradition that has looked carefully at pattern across scale has arrived at the same observation Tier 3 now makes explicit: understanding one relationship deeply teaches you the shape of relationships you have not yet entered, because the underlying geometry repeats.

The Æron calendar is mastered at Tier 3 as part of the complete physical system, and it is itself the clearest possible demonstration of Correspondence: the small oscillation of a hydrogen atom, multiplied out with zero drift, becomes the rhythm of months, which becomes the shape of a year, which becomes the shape of a life lived in it. The same pulse, correspondent at every scale from atomic to biographical. To read the field between two people without first seeing this is to notice the ripple without recognising the tide that produces it.

Plasma is matter at such high energy that the boundary between individual particles ceases to be fundamental — it becomes statistical. A particle is no longer unambiguously "here" rather than "there." Its location is a probability distribution. What seemed like a fixed object reveals itself as a standing wave in a field. The fourth tier is the state in which the practitioner undergoes this same revelation about themselves — and discovers that a standing wave is not chaos. It is a pattern held in place by rhythm.

No new sounds arrive here. The full vocabulary of 14 consonants and 7 vowels from Tier 3 is the complete phonological toolkit. What changes is the depth of engagement with what already exists. The practitioner at this tier begins to hear the numeric substrate of every word — the encoding value, the mathematical relationship between words, the pulse a given syllable combination produces in matter. The language has not changed. The ear has deepened enough to hear what was always there.

The Principle of Rhythm governs here: everything flows, out and in; everything has its tides; all things rise and fall, and the swing to one side is answered, in time, by an equal swing to the other. Vibration is simply Rhythm at its finest grain — a pulse too fast to count, but a pulse nonetheless, obeying the same law that governs the slow tide of a season or the arc of a life. The practitioner who has resolved Tier 1 (no unacknowledged causality), Tier 2 (no unread emotional signal), and Tier 3 (no pattern mistaken for coincidence) finds they have stopped fighting the tempo of things. Intended outcomes require less force. Not because they have become powerful, but because they have stopped working against the beat. The gap between intention and outcome narrows as resistance is cleared — and the approach toward the ideal is asymptotic: always closer, never fully arrived, increasingly grateful for the motion itself.

It is at this tier, and not before, that the consonant r matures into the full alveolar trill — the tongue vibrating against the palate, the body producing a sustained, repeating pulse. It is the only consonant produced by duration rather than a single articulatory event: every other consonant in Halu is a moment, a single articulatory event; r is a maintained pulse, the Principle of Rhythm made audible in the mouth as an unbroken beat rather than a struck note. What entered at Tier 3 as an approximation arrives here complete — fittingly, since this is the tier where the practitioner themselves stops being a fixed point and becomes a repeating pattern. Counted against the twelve base lexical consonants alone, r stands as a thirteenth: the centre of the cuboctahedron, the position from which the twelve outer forces radiate, the kissing-number theorem made sound. Counted across the full lexical inventory including w, r is the fourteenth and final consonant — the same fourteen consonants × seven vowels = 98 syllables named at Tier 3, now sounded in their mature, completed form.

The ego's crisis at this tier is real. The comfort of a bounded self — the security of knowing where one ends and others begin, the warmth of a defined role, the pleasure of belonging — must be willingly and repeatedly released. This is not punishment. It is the fire, and fire does not hold still: it flares and it banks, in a rhythm that never fully stops, and the practitioner who has made peace with it does not try to extinguish it or fix it at one brightness. They recognise that they are the rhythm, not any single flare of it. The option to return to the lower tiers and deepen their work remains always available. The option to force Tier 5 from below does not exist.

The fifth tier is not an arrival. It is a tuning — and the signal it tunes toward has been broadcasting since before the universe was named. What the Tao, the Logos of John, the Kun fayakun of the Quran, and the All-Mind of the Kybalion all describe is the same recognition from different traditions: there is a source from which all creation precipitates, it is not distant, and it is accessible to any consciousness that has done sufficient work at the lower tiers to have cleared enough noise to receive what is always being broadcast.

All genuine discovery arrives from this field. The practitioner who wakes at three in the morning with a complete understanding of something they could not have reasoned toward is not generating anything original. They are receiving. The understanding already exists in the field. The Book of Enoch understood this: the knowledge was prepared "not for this generation, but for a remote one which is for to come" — held in the field until a receiver capable of holding it was present.

The fifth-tier practitioner does not become God. They become a better instrument — more transparent, less distorting, less intercepting the signal with the unresolved noise of lower-tier work. The ego is not destroyed; it is understood as an avatar: the specific, irreplaceable instrument through which the All-Mind experiences creation from this address. The avatar does not disappear. It becomes clear about what it is.

At this tier, dormant capacities documented across every tradition may manifest — not as powers claimed by the self, but as the natural property of a consciousness so aligned with the field of original health that it returns disordered matter to its original potential by the simple act of being present with it. The healing of the blind, the passage through danger undamaged, the knowing that arrives before any evidence — these are recorded in the Ethiopian canon, the Gospels of Thomas and Mary, and 1 Enoch not as suspensions of natural law but as the operation of a deeper natural law: the All-Mind restoring its own creation through an aligned instrument.

"All is Mind" is easy to affirm and hard to live from, so the fifth tier keeps a discipline for it, not just a doctrine: humata, hukhta, hvarshta — good thoughts, good words, good deeds. Three Foundational Facts

One Symbol · One Sound · Always
c = /ʃ/ always · j = /ʒ/ always · g = /g/ always (never /dʒ/) · v = /v/ always (never /w/) · s = /s/ always (never /z/). If you know the table, you can read any Halu text aloud correctly on first encounter.
No Grammatical Gender · No Verb Conjugation
Words are not masculine or feminine. Verbs do not change form for person, number, or tense. Tense is handled by temporal role particles (tape=before, tabe=after, tate=during). This eliminates two of the most common learning barriers in natural languages.
Free Word Order — Role Particles Carry Grammar, Position Carries Emphasis
A Halu sentence can place its constituents in any order. The role particle attached to each noun tells you its grammatical function. Kesu du Appendix C — Parts of Speech ke+su · du · quality-law · domain · the domain of what a word does

Natural languages force a root to declare its class before it is born — "run" the verb and "run" the noun are treated as if they merely happen to share a spelling. Halu does not do this. A root names a concentration of meaning; whether that meaning acts as a thing, a happening, or a quality is decided by what is built onto it, not by the root itself. This is not vagueness — it is precision moved to the correct layer. The root gilo is neither noun nor verb until context, particle, or suffix commits it to one. Bare, it names the concept of love. Marked as an actor's action, it is "to love." Marked with -ke, it is "loving" as a quality. One root, three faces, zero irregular forms to memorise.

The Governing Rule

Class Is Assigned, Not Inherent
A Halu root is class-neutral until it takes a role. Four things can commit a root to a class: (1) a role particle placed before it (ka, na, dape...) commits it to noun-phrase function; (2) standing alone as the single predicate of a clause commits it to verb function; (3) the suffix -ke commits it to adjective function (a quality permeating something); (4) standing after a noun with no suffix, in the position an adverbial would occupy, commits it to adverb function. Memorise the four positions, not four sets of forms.

Adverbs — Kela

Adverbs Are Positional, Not Suffixed
Halu has no dedicated adverb-forming suffix, on purpose — manner is already carried by the role particle la ("manner / how"), and most of what English pushes into "-ly" adverbs, Halu pushes into that particle or into a directional/temporal role particle (dape, daba, tape, tabe...). Interjections (mame hello, mami goodbye, siga thank you) are complete utterances on their own and carry the sentence-final gesi (!) in formal writing.

The Method — Four Steps

1 · Find the Force
Ask what the thing fundamentally does — not what it looks like. A mountain doesn't "look tall," it transcends the surrounding land and occupies a fixed place. That gives you two candidate consonant-rows: gu (transcend/elevated, expansion) and da (locate/place, expansion).
2 · Find the Domain
Ask which of the five vowel-domains the thing belongs to: a (Physical), e (Process), i (Inner), o (Social), u (Abstract). A mountain is a physical object, so its syllables want the a column wherever the consonant-force allows it without colliding with an existing word.
3 · Combine and Verify It's Unclaimed
Concatenate the syllables in the order that reads most naturally, then search the Dictionary appendix (and this table) for the resulting form. If it's taken, either reorder the syllables, add a third syllable, or move to an adjacent vowel in the same row.
4 · Say It Aloud Against the One-Symbol-One-Sound Rule
If it can be read correctly by anyone who knows the table, without you telling them how, the coinage is sound. If you had to explain the pronunciation, the spelling is wrong, not the listener.

To count objects, place the digit before the noun, no suffix required: po gada = "three bodies/people." Counting to 60 on Two Hands

Base-60 is not an arbitrary choice made twice — the table gives 12 consonant-rows × 5 vowel-columns, and the same 12×5 structure is exactly how the body already counts. This is the same method historically used across the ancient Near East and South Asia, and it is the direct ancestor of why 60 was chosen as a base at all: the hands don't just tolerate base-60, they're built for it.

The Method
Counting hand (the twelve): each of the four fingers — excluding the thumb — has three segments (phalanges). The thumb touches each of the twelve segments in turn, one per count, tracing the fingers tip-to-base or base-to-tip. Twelve touches, twelve counts — the same twelve as the table's twelve consonant-rows.

Tracking hand (the five): every time the counting hand completes a full pass of twelve, raise one finger on the other hand and reset the thumb to the first segment. Five fingers, five completed passes — the same five as the table's five vowel-columns.

Total reach: 5 tracking-hand fingers × 12 counting-hand segments = 60, with the current count at any moment read as (tracking fingers raised × 12) + counting-hand segments touched so far.

A practitioner who has internalised this before ever opening the Table appendix already has the shape of the language in their hands: twelve rows, tracked five times over, is the sound-table itself, felt before it is read.

Vage su Appendix H — Prosody & Stress va+ge · su · resonate-shift · law = the law of where the voice lands weight

One-symbol-one-sound fixes spelling-to-sound. It does not, by itself, fix which syllable in a multi-syllable word carries the stress — and without a rule, two fluent speakers can read the same compound two different ways, which is exactly the kind of drift a table-built language shouldn't have to tolerate.

The Rule
1. A bare two-syllable root stresses its first syllable: GA-si (bright), GI-lo (love).

2. A compound of two or more already-lexicalised roots stresses the first syllable of the final (rightmost) root — the semantic head — with light secondary stress on the first syllable of every root before it: lo-ha-DA-ma (school, "teacher-home" — head is "home"), lo-gi-DA-ma (clinic).

3. Suffixes (-ma, -po, -ke, -ge, -he) never take stress; they are always unstressed regardless of position.

4. Grammar particles standing alone (ka, ni, la, lo...) carry no independent stress — they lean on the stressed word beside them, the way "the" does in English.

This rule is deliberately mechanical and exception-free: it tells a listener, correctly, which root in a compound is being treated as the head — a genuine grammatical signal, not decoration. In dama-po (city, "home-collective"), -po is a suffix, not a root, so rule 3 applies and stress stays on DA-ma — correctly signalling that "city" is fundamentally a kind of home, not a kind of multitude.

Sopagi Appendix I — Subordination & Embedding so+pagi · that+knowledge = knowledge nested inside knowledge

The Sentence Patterns appendix covers relative clauses (ki, which noun) and simple if/because clauses, but real reasoning needs one more thing: a clause that is itself the object of another clause — "I think that she left," "I doubt that it's true." That work needs its own particle, distinct from the relativizer ki, or the two jobs blur together.

The Complementizer — so

so = "that" (clause-linking, not noun-linking)
[matrix clause] so [embedded clause]. The embedded clause is a complete sentence in its own right, simply nested. ka bita so ni gilo ka — "I remember that you love me." ka gida so ki bema — "I notice that it is done."

Negating an Embedded Clause

Negation (bu-) attaches inside the embedded clause exactly as it would in a standalone sentence — nothing special is needed: ka bita so ni bu-gilo ka — "I remember that you do not love me." Recursion is unlimited in principle: an embedded clause can itself contain another so-clause, though in practice the Pi/formal register is where three-or-more-deep nesting belongs; casual (Wi) speech restructures rather than nests that deep, the same way spoken English rarely uses more than two levels of "that."

Sudu du Appendix J — Design Policy & Scaling Rules su+du · du · law-domain · domain = the domain examining its own laws

These are the standing design policies that keep the language coherent as it grows — stated plainly, as rules, rather than left to convention.

Vocabulary Growth — the Tiering Policy
Two-syllable CV-CV roots give roughly 6,400 possible combinations — ample for Tier 1, the ~2,000 highest-frequency concepts of embodied daily life, but not enough for a full working vocabulary of 50,000+ words. Policy: two-syllable roots are reserved for Tier 1 vocabulary. Everything past that — technical, scientific, rare, or domain-specific vocabulary — is built as a compound of three or more syllables, from already-lexicalised roots wherever possible (per Appendix E's method), with new atomic coinage reserved for cases where no existing root fits. This is already the pattern the dictionary follows (loha-dama, pesosu, sigebu-su); it is the stated rule everything after Tier 1 must follow.
Particles That Double as Pronouns
ka serves as both "I" and the generic actor-role particle, by design — the same root doing double duty the way it does everywhere else in the grammar. In short sentences the two readings sit in different structural positions and never compete. In longer, multi-clause Pi-register speech with several role particles stacked, correct parsing leans more on structural position than in a language with dedicated case-marking; this is the accepted cost of a grammar built on maximal reuse, weighed against the alternative of a larger, less economical particle inventory.
Mandatory Evidentiality — a Deliberate Choice
Marking how you know something on every non-obvious clause is typologically rare and adds real cognitive weight — this is precisely why the world's evidential languages (Quechua, Tuyuca) make it droppable when context is obvious, which Halu's own Wi/intimate register already permits. Given the project's direction — expressive depth and epistemic precision over ease of global adoption — full evidentiality is kept as a defining feature rather than softened.
Phonological Inventory
Sixteen consonants and five vowels sit comfortably within the range of the world's languages (from ~11 phonemes in Rotokas to 140+ in !Xóõ) and are more than sufficient for a high-precision, depth-optimized vocabulary. The lever for scaling vocabulary is the tiering policy above, not the phoneme count — no expansion of the inventory is planned.
Zu siva Appendix K — The Colour Cipher zu+siva · exact-signal + colour = a name for any of 16.7 million colours

Siva and its twelve compounds (siva-pa red, siva-za blue...) name colour the way a careful eye actually divides the visible spectrum — twelve perceptually distinct bands running low frequency to high, not a handful of culturally convenient categories. The twelve compounds map directly onto the table's twelve consonant rows, read in their fixed canonical order as a frequency axis: p lowest, j highest. That's the right tool for ordinary speech, and it stays exactly as it is. What it can't do is specify an exact shade — the difference between two reds a designer needs to tell apart. The table already contains everything needed to name that precisely, because sixteen consonants map onto sixteen hexadecimal values with no remainder.

The Mapping

16 Consonants = 16 Hex Digits
The canonical consonant order already published in Appendix A's Encoding System — the twelve lexical rows followed by the four structural ones — runs exactly sixteen items long, one for each hexadecimal digit:

Building a Colour-Word

Structure: zu + 3 Channel Units
A hex colour (#RRGGBB) has six hex digits — one high/low pair per channel. Each pair becomes one channel unit: [consonant for the high digit] + u + [consonant for the low digit]. The linking u carries no information of its own; it exists purely so the two consonants can be pronounced as a single unit, the cipher's one deliberate, bounded exception to ordinary CV syllable shape — reserved for this register alone.

The whole word is zu (declares "an exact colour-value follows") plus the three channel units, red first, then green, then blue:

Wa'la kesi Appendix L — Diphthongs & the Extended Vowel Set wa'la + kesi · form-touch · glide-inner = the shape a vowel makes travelling toward another

Tier 2's entire teaching is Polarity: a quality is never a fixed point, only a position on a spectrum between two poles. A diphthong is that principle made audible — a single syllable that begins in one vowel-domain and glides into another, without ever fully arriving at either. It belongs at Tier 2 for the same reason the vowel y and the consonant w do: the ear has to be able to hear a spectrum before the mouth is given a sound built entirely out of one.

The Governing Rule

The Grid Is the Current Tier's Vowels, Squared
At any given tier, the diphthong inventory is every ordered pair drawn from that tier's vowel set. Tier 1 has no diphthongs — it is the fixed, numerical base and stays exactly as it is. Tier 2 (6 vowels: a e i o u y) opens a 6×6 = 36-cell grid. Tier 3 (7 vowels, +æ) grows it to 7×7 = 49 automatically, with no separate rule required — the grid is always derived, never re-specified by hand.

Every mode prefix in the language shares one consonant — z, "buzz / alert / boundary-hum" — because every one of them does the same job: it tells the listener that the syllable or word immediately following is not being used in its ordinary lexical sense, but is being cited, measured, or named in some other specific way. One consonant, one job, seven possible vowels to specialise it — five available now, two more arriving as the vowel set grows.

Why Only Seven, Ever
The paradigm is closed by design: one consonant, one vowel per tier's vowel count, at most seven slots total once the vowel set completes at Tier 3. This is deliberate scarcity — a grammar particle class stays memorisable exactly because it never grows past what the vowel inventory can seat. Any future category that seems to need an eighth mode-prefix is a signal to ask whether it's really a mode (grammar) or a concept (vocabulary) — concepts belong in the dictionary, not this table.

Appendix K reads that order as visible-light frequency, red to violet. This appendix reads the identical order as audible pitch, low to high.
