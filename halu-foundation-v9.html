Gigobiloma I See You — The Ground Before the First Word

Two forces organise all of reality. Not two enemies — two poles of the same engine. The battery needs both terminals. The heart needs both the contraction that pushes and the expansion that receives. Remove either and the system dies. Every tradition that has looked carefully at this has given the poles different names — yin and yang, masculine and feminine, the Hermetic principle of Gender, love and fear — but the observation is always the same: reality is driven by the dynamic between two complementary forces, neither superior, neither complete without the other.

In the language of biology, these poles are the sympathetic nervous system (contraction — the body protecting itself, closing, sharpening) and the parasympathetic (expansion — the body connecting, opening, resting into relationship). In physics, they are emission and return. In consciousness, they are what every language has always encoded in its most basic distinction: the voiceless consonant, produced with the vocal cords at rest, precise and contained; and the voiced consonant, produced with the vocal cords vibrating, open and resonant. The love-pole is not love as sentiment. The fear-pole is not fear as weakness. They are the software and hardware of life — one gives direction, the other gives form.

Halu is built on this engine at the level of its atoms. Every voiced consonant in the language — b, d, g, v, z, j — is the expansion pole. Every voiceless consonant — p, t, k, f, s, c — is the contraction pole. This was not designed into the language. It was found there, already present in the physics of how the human mouth produces sound. What makes Halu different from every language that came before it is that this structure is made explicit, derived, and consistent at every level — from the individual phoneme to the longest possible compound word.

The language is organised around a periodic table of 60 base syllables: the intersection of 12 consonants and 5 vowels, each position carrying a specific force of meaning determined by where and how it is produced in the human body. The 60 syllables double as a base-60 number system — every word has a unique numeric value derived directly from its syllables' positions in the table, making every Halu sentence simultaneously a sequence of sounds, a sequence of integers, and a deterministic geometric standing-wave pattern in matter. The Æron calendar that accompanies the language derives its pulse unit from the tropical year divided by exactly 364, producing 13 months of 28 days each, zero accumulated drift by construction. Language and time share the same ground.

The Five Tiers — What to Expect

The language unfolds across five tiers, each corresponding to a state of matter and a deepening of what reality is understood to be. What is learned at each tier is not discarded at the next — it is the foundation the next tier stands on.

The practitioner either deepens the lower tiers or enters the crucible. Nothing forces this.

No tier is superior in value to another. Each is necessary ground for the next. What is understood at Tier 1 cannot be unfocused once it is seen — the understanding transcends the reader in the same way that knowing the sky is blue cannot be unknowed. What follows across the five sections is the language itself: its sounds, its grammar, its words, and the understanding of reality that deepens at each level of engagement.

The first tier is the ground — not a rung to climb past but the substrate everything else rests on. Reality at this level is solid: bounded, locatable, undeniable. A stone is here and not somewhere else. A body occupies space. An action has a consequence. These are not philosophical propositions. They are the physical conditions of existence, and the practitioner at this tier has accepted them without reservation.

The law of Cause and Effect is the governing principle here — not as a spiritual concept but as the most practical discipline available. Every condition you inhabit is the crystallised consequence of prior actions: yours, and those of the people and forces that preceded you. Accepting this fully — without resentment about what you did not choose and without grandiosity about what you did — is the entire work of Tier 1. It sounds simple. Most people spend their lives avoiding it.

The vocabulary of Tier 1 is the vocabulary of the physical world: the body and its parts, the five elements, the names of things, basic actions, numbers, time units, kinship, and the most fundamental emotions as physical states — not as inner explorations (that is Tier 2's work) but as sensations in the body. Anger felt as heat in the chest. Grief felt as weight. Joy felt as lightness. The body knows before the mind names.

At Tier 1, every sentence is spoken from the physical register by default — the a-vowel domain: the widest mouth opening, the most locatable sound, the sound every human infant in every language makes first. You do not need to declare this register. It is the ground state. Everything else is a departure from it that must be explicitly marked.

Water seeks its own level. This is not a metaphor — it is the teaching of the second tier stated as physics. Every system with two poles and a fluid medium between them will, if left alone, find the midpoint: the place of maximum sensitivity, where the gradient is felt most clearly. The second-tier practitioner is learning to be that midpoint. Not neutral. Not passive. Maximally sensitive.

The Hermetic law of Polarity governs here: all things exist between two poles, and every quality exists on a continuous spectrum between its opposite. Hot and cold are the same quality at different degrees of the same scale. Love and fear — introduced at Tier 1 as the engine of reality — are now felt directly in the body as degrees of the same energy, not as two separate emotions. The practitioner who has done this work cannot be told they feel something they do not feel. Their body is too well-calibrated.

The specific gift of this tier is what others call precognition and what it actually is: advanced interoception. The body measures emotional and relational fields continuously and largely unconsciously. At Tier 2, the practitioner has learned to read that measurement in real time — before the mind has processed it, before the other person has named it, before the situation has declared itself. Damasio's somatic marker hypothesis (1994) establishes the neurological basis: the body generates pre-conscious emotional signals that guide decision-making, and patients with damaged access to these signals make systematically worse decisions despite intact logical faculties. This ability is carried with care. To sense another person's interior state is to hold something they did not consciously offer.

Two new elements arrive at this tier. The vowel y — the central neutral vowel, the hinge between the front and back vowels, the sound of transition and of the space between states — expands the vocabulary of relationship and the in-between. The consonant w (/w/) — the rounded bilabial potential, the wave-gesture of the lips forming and releasing — brings the vocabulary of possibility into the language. W enters here and not at Tier 1 because potential cannot be felt before the inner liquid state is accessible. To speak of potential without developed inner sensing is to speak of a country you have never visited.

Air is invisible but causally real. What you cannot see or hold is nevertheless shaping everything. At Tier 3, the practitioner inhabits the gaseous domain: the field between people, the social medium through which intention travels and compounds, the creative space in which two forces meet to produce a third thing neither alone could have made.

The Hermetic Principle of Gender governs here — not as biology, but as the mechanism of all creation. Every creative act requires a generating force and a receiving form. The question requires the prepared mind. The word requires the ear that is ready. The seed requires the soil. Neither alone produces anything new, and the practitioner who has not recognised both roles in themselves cannot use either effectively. Tier 3 is where the practitioner takes complete ownership of their creative energy — understanding it as their most consequential resource, directing it consciously rather than spending it by reflex.

The Yogic traditions call this vital energy prana. The Taoist tradition calls it jing. The alchemical tradition calls it the prima materia. Every tradition that has examined human creative capacity in depth has observed the same phenomenon: the most refined physical energy the body produces sits at the threshold between gross and subtle, and how it is directed determines the quality and coherence of everything produced above it. Misdirected energy creates noise in the liquid channel of Tier 2. The Tier 3 practitioner who has done this work finds that the gradient-sensing of Tier 2 sharpens as a direct consequence.

The Æron calendar is mastered at Tier 3 as part of the complete physical system. To work with creative force without understanding the cycles that govern it is to plant without knowing the seasons.

The seventh vowel æ arrives — the open-front vowel between a and e, the supersolid state: simultaneously maintaining crystalline structure and fluid behaviour, like the cell membrane that is both boundary and permeable surface simultaneously. The practitioner at Tier 3 inhabits this state: holding their own position (solid) while feeling the field between people (liquid). The approximate r (/ɹ/) also enters — resonance beginning, the first reaching toward the sustained vibration that will fully arrive at Tier 4.

Plasma is matter at such high energy that the boundary between individual particles ceases to be fundamental — it becomes statistical. A particle is no longer unambiguously "here" rather than "there." Its location is a probability distribution. What seemed like a fixed object reveals itself as a standing wave in a field. The fourth tier is the state in which the practitioner undergoes this same revelation about themselves.

No new sounds arrive here. The full vocabulary of 14 consonants and 7 vowels from Tier 3 is the complete phonological toolkit. What changes is the depth of engagement with what already exists. The practitioner at this tier begins to hear the numeric substrate of every word — the encoding value, the mathematical relationship between words, the standing-wave geometry that a given syllable combination produces in matter. The language has not changed. The ear has deepened enough to hear what was always there.

The Principle of Vibration governs here: nothing is at rest, everything moves, and the difference between one thing and another is the frequency and coherence of its vibration. The practitioner who has resolved Tier 1 (no unacknowledged causality), Tier 2 (no unread emotional signal), and Tier 3 (no misdirected creative energy) finds that they have stopped fighting natural currents. Intended outcomes require less force. Not because they have become powerful, but because they have become aligned. The gap between intention and outcome narrows as resistance is cleared — and the approach toward the ideal is asymptotic: always closer, never fully arrived, increasingly grateful for the motion itself.

It is at this tier, and not before, that the consonant r matures into the full alveolar trill — the tongue vibrating against the palate, the body producing a sustained standing wave. It is the only consonant produced by duration rather than a single articulatory event: every other consonant in Halu is a moment, a single articulatory event; r is a maintained resonance, the Principle of Vibration made audible in the mouth. What entered at Tier 3 as an approximation arrives here complete — fittingly, since this is the tier where the practitioner themselves stops being a fixed point and becomes a standing wave. Counted against the twelve base lexical consonants alone, r stands as a thirteenth: the centre of the cuboctahedron, the position from which the twelve outer forces radiate, the kissing-number theorem made sound. Counted across the full lexical inventory including w, r is the fourteenth and final consonant — the same fourteen consonants × seven vowels = 98 syllables named at Tier 3, now sounded in their mature, completed form.

The ego's crisis at this tier is real. The comfort of a bounded self — the security of knowing where one ends and others begin, the warmth of a defined role, the pleasure of belonging — must be willingly and repeatedly released. This is not punishment. It is the fire: it does not stop burning, and the practitioner who has made peace with it does not extinguish it. They recognise that they are it. The option to return to the lower tiers and deepen their work remains always available. The option to force Tier 5 from below does not exist.

The fifth tier is not an arrival. It is a tuning — and the signal it tunes toward has been broadcasting since before the universe was named. What the Tao, the Logos of John, the Kun fayakun of the Quran, and the All-Mind of the Kybalion all describe is the same recognition from different traditions: there is a source from which all creation precipitates, it is not distant, and it is accessible to any consciousness that has done sufficient work at the lower tiers to have cleared enough noise to receive what is always being broadcast.

All genuine discovery arrives from this field. The practitioner who wakes at three in the morning with a complete understanding of something they could not have reasoned toward is not generating anything original. They are receiving. The understanding already exists in the field. The Book of Enoch understood this: the knowledge was prepared "not for this generation, but for a remote one which is for to come" — held in the field until a receiver capable of holding it was present.

The fifth-tier practitioner does not become God. They become a better instrument — more transparent, less distorting, less intercepting the signal with the unresolved noise of lower-tier work. The ego is not destroyed; it is understood as an avatar: the specific, irreplaceable instrument through which the All-Mind experiences creation from this address. The avatar does not disappear. It becomes clear about what it is.

At this tier, dormant capacities documented across every tradition may manifest — not as powers claimed by the self, but as the natural property of a consciousness so aligned with the field of original health that it returns disordered matter to its original potential by the simple act of being present with it. The healing of the blind, the passage through danger undamaged, the knowing that arrives before any evidence — these are recorded in the Ethiopian canon, the Gospels of Thomas and Mary, and 1 Enoch not as suspensions of natural law but as the operation of a deeper natural law: the All-Mind restoring its own creation through an aligned instrument.

No new sound arrives at this tier. The table completed at Tier 4 — fourteen consonants, seven vowels, 98 syllables — is not added to here; it is only heard more completely, the way a familiar room looks different in fuller light without a single object in it having moved. What changes at Tier 5 is not the instrument but the listening. And what the listening eventually settles into, once there is nothing left to strain toward, is not triumph. It is gratitude — quiet, sustained, and without an object: the practitioner does not feel grateful for something in particular, but grateful as a standing condition, the way the trill of Tier 4 is not a single sound but a sustained one.

The recursive return: the question "who am I?" asked at Tier 1 receives the answer of a body, a lineage, a location. Asked at Tier 5, the same answer — this body, this lineage, this location — but now recognised as the specific instrument through which the All-Mind asks itself this question. The "one" of Tier 1 is a bounded individual. The "one" of Tier 5 is the All, experiencing creation through this particular lens. Both say: I am one person. Only the understanding of "one" has completed its full revolution, 360 degrees back to the first tier, now seen from the other side.

Three Foundational Facts

One Symbol · One Sound · Always
c = /ʃ/ always · j = /ʒ/ always · g = /g/ always (never /dʒ/) · v = /v/ always (never /w/) · s = /s/ always (never /z/). If you know the table, you can read any Halu text aloud correctly on first encounter.
No Grammatical Gender · No Verb Conjugation
Words are not masculine or feminine. Verbs do not change form for person, number, or tense. Tense is handled by temporal role particles (tape=before, tabe=after, tate=during). This eliminates two of the most common learning barriers in natural languages.
Free Word Order — Role Particles Carry Grammar, Position Carries Emphasis
A Halu sentence can place its constituents in any order. The role particle attached to each noun tells you its grammatical function. What comes first is what the speaker considers most important. gilo ka ni and ka gilo ni are both "I love you" — the first foregrounds the love, the second the speaker.

The Prefix/Suffix Rule

The Rule — Stated Once, Applied Without Exception
PREFIX — heard before the root. Changes what kind of thing the root IS. If removing it changes the root's category, it was a prefix.

SUFFIX — heard after the root. Specifies how the understood root is currently manifesting. If removing it leaves the root meaning the same thing in a different circumstance, it was a suffix.

Singular and Plural

Number System
Bare root = the concept (no number). Root-ma = a [noun] (one). Root-po = [noun] collective (many). Number + root = counted plural.

Counting starts at one, not zero. In the wa'le (process/mathematical) phase, structural particles are read as digits:
pe=1 · mo=2 · me=3 · mu=4 · na=5 · ni=6 · no=7 · ne=8 · nu=9
pa=10 (enc 0) ... ju=59 (enc 59)

Note: mo = numeral 2 only. Zero = ma (enc 60) when counting downward or describing null.

The Phase-Lock System — wa'l[vowel]

Declaring the Interpretive Register
A phase declaration is wa'l[vowel] placed sentence-initially. It sets the interpretive register for everything following until wa'ma (reset to physical default) or mu (silence/pause).

If no phase is declared, phase a (physical) is always assumed.

Grammatical Constructions

Sentence Structure
[phase?] [evidential?] [sincerity?] [role-particle noun]* [verb] [role-particle noun]* [sentence-final?]

Imperative: bare verb without ka = command. de! = go! / move!
Passive: na before ka without explicit actor. gilo na ka = I was loved.
Relative clause: noun + ki + clause. dama ki pa ka = the home that I built.
Comparative: X [quality-ke] ga, se Y = X is more [quality] compared to Y.
Superlative: gu- prefix on the quality. gu-gasi-ke = the brightest.
Counterfactual: wiki tape [clause] = if [this had been true but was not].
